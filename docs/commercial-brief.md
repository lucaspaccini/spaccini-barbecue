## 1. Executive summary

Spaccini Barbecue should be positioned as a live barbecue experience and a source of long-cooked preparations, not as a generic catering catalogue. The strongest commercial narrative connects three elements:

1. Roberto Spaccini developed the cooking system he now uses.
2. The food is prepared through controlled, long cooking and can become a visible part of an event.
3. Each request is evaluated around the event, location, expected attendance, service format, and preparation rather than forced into an invented fixed package.

The website must quickly help a visitor answer four questions:

- Is this suitable for my event or pickup need?
- What makes it different from a conventional catering proposal?
- Can I trust the person, method, and organization behind it?
- What information do I need to provide to receive a concrete answer?

The commercial system should move users from local search, social content, referrals, or event exposure toward a low-friction availability request. Frequent calls to action are appropriate, but persuasion must be grounded in real service details, authentic visual proof, documented history, and transparent handling of unknowns.

The first commercial release should prioritize clarity and qualified enquiries over breadth. It should not publish unverified prices, capacities, provenance claims, patent claims, testimonials, availability promises, or health claims.

## 2. Commercial objective

### Primary objective

Generate qualified requests for event availability and estimates in Perugia and Umbria.

A qualified event request should eventually provide enough context to start a useful conversation: indicative date, place, event type, approximate attendance, desired service, and a contact method.

### Secondary objectives

- Generate enquiries for preparation booking and pickup.
- Explain the service before contact so Roberto spends less time resolving basic uncertainty.
- Build trust through documented history, visible process, authentic events, and verifiable proof.
- Make the differences between live event service, pickup, and a conventional catering offer understandable.
- Establish a measurable acquisition and conversion baseline that can be improved over time.
- Support local organic discovery without creating thin or duplicated location pages.

### Non-objectives for the first commercial release

- Online sales or payment
- Automatic availability or instant quotation
- A fixed menu or price list without operational validation
- A marketplace, recipe publication, or barbecue equipment shop
- Guaranteed search rankings or unsubstantiated performance claims

## 3. Services and operating model

### 3.1 Events with live cooking

Potential event types include birthdays, weddings, graduations, corporate events, festivals, wineries, breweries, private parties, and other events evaluated individually.

The working service model is:

- initial operating area: Perugia and Umbria;
- out-of-region availability: evaluated case by case;
- no fixed minimum or maximum attendance currently approved for publication;
- deposit, minimum price, price range, lead time, and payment schedule depend on organization and menu;
- possible equipment: barbecue, stand, cooking area, and the equipment required for the agreed service;
- possible formats: preparation on site, visible cooking, buffet, or immediate sandwich preparation and handoff near the barbecue area;
- final scope: confirmed through a tailored proposal.

The customer is not only buying food. The intended value is relief from managing fire, meat, equipment, and long timings, combined with a preparation guests can see, smell, and experience. This value must still be supported by real photography, event records, and clear operational boundaries.

### 3.2 Booking and pickup

The current working model is:

- preparations can be booked and collected at the business location;
- no fixed collection days are approved for publication;
- fixed selections or variable requests may be possible;
- lead time depends on the preparation;
- booking currently takes place by phone or in person;
- payment normally occurs at pickup unless a specific arrangement is agreed;
- availability, quantity, timing, and price are confirmed case by case.

Before this service is published, confirm the official address, collection instructions, approved contact details, typical lead-time ranges, payment methods, and whether any recurring offer can be stated reliably.

## 5. Problems solved

The website should connect each service to practical progress for the customer:

- find an alternative to a conventional catering format;
- give guests a visible and recognizable food moment;
- avoid personally managing fire, equipment, meat, and cooking schedules;
- serve long and complex preparations without organizing an internal kitchen;
- combine food service with part of the event experience;
- assess a proposal around venue, quantity, and event type;
- rely on the person who developed and uses the cooking system;
- book preparations that are difficult or time-consuming to make at home;
- reduce uncertainty through a clear enquiry checklist and response process.

The site must not imply that every venue, group size, preparation, or date is feasible. Its job is to make evaluation easier and set the expectation that feasibility is confirmed case by case.

## 9. Cooking system

### Working technical inventory

Historical proprietary documentation describes:

- pellet fuel;
- possible use of wood or wood chips;
- automated ignition;
- temperature control;
- long cooking autonomy;
- stainless-steel construction;
- barbecue, grill, smoker, and oven functions;
- a cooking chamber that can be organized on multiple levels;
- heat diffusers;
- no direct contact between food and flame;
- external collection of cooking liquids;
- controlled long-cooking capability;
- an XL model intended in part for events.

These are not all publication-ready. Each must be checked against the equipment currently used, current operating procedures, original manuals or drawings, and the owner's technical confirmation.

### Explanation hierarchy

1. **Mechanical fact:** food is separated from direct flame and cooking liquids are directed outside rather than falling directly onto the heat source beneath the food.
2. **Operational consequence:** the design avoids direct combustion of those liquids below the food and supports temperature-managed long cooks.
3. **Reasonable gastronomic description:** Roberto can manage time, temperature, and the selected aromatic contribution for the intended preparation.
4. **General scientific context:** literature identifies fat dripping onto flames and the resulting smoke as one pathway associated with PAH formation in conventional grilling.
5. **Product-specific health conclusion:** unavailable; would require a defined test protocol and analysis of the actual equipment, fuels, foods, temperatures, and procedures.

### Publication-safe scientific wording

> Il sistema separa gli alimenti dalla fiamma e convoglia all'esterno grassi e liquidi di cottura, evitando che cadano direttamente sulla fonte di calore sotto la carne. La letteratura generale identifica il gocciolamento dei grassi sulla fiamma come una delle condizioni che possono contribuire alla formazione di fumo e IPA. Non sono disponibili analisi specifiche che dimostrino un beneficio sanitario del sistema Spaccini.

The second sentence should appear publicly only if the final content strategy genuinely needs scientific context; the mechanical explanation is clearer and lower risk for most visitors.

### Prohibited claims

- eliminates harmful substances;
- free from PAHs or HCAs;
- healthier or less toxic;
- always more digestible;
- clinically or scientifically proven health benefit;
- guaranteed superiority to competitors;
- patented, until patent number, owner, territory, and status are verified.

## 13. Conversion strategy

### Intended user path

1. **Entry:** local search, social profile, referral, venue link, or event exposure.
2. **Recognition:** immediate explanation of live barbecue for events and pickup in Perugia and Umbria.
3. **Fit:** service type, example occasions, operating area, and case-by-case model.
4. **Value:** why live preparation and long cooking matter to the host and guests.
5. **Trust:** Roberto's documented history, current process, authentic images, events, and verified reviews.
6. **Objection handling:** logistics, numbers, lead time, weather, pricing process, and pickup.
7. **Action:** low-commitment availability request.
8. **Confirmation:** clear acknowledgement, expected response time, and a copy or summary of the request.
9. **Human follow-up:** Roberto or an authorized person confirms feasibility and collects missing detail.

### Future initial enquiry fields

- name;
- preferred contact method and contact detail;
- indicative date;
- place or municipality;
- event type;
- approximate number of people;
- desired service;
- optional message.

Do not ask for a full event specification at first contact. Use conditional follow-up questions only when they materially affect feasibility.

### Microcopy principles

- Present the first request as exploratory and non-binding.
- Explain why date, place, and indicative attendance are requested.
- Label optional fields clearly.
- State the expected response time only after it is operationally confirmed.
- Never display fake urgency or unverified availability.
- Preserve entered data when validation fails.
- Use plain Italian and concrete labels instead of marketing jargon.

### Alternative contact routes

- Instagram `@bbq.you` is the only currently verified public contact and is the temporary destination for the homepage enquiry CTA.
- A dedicated commercial telephone number remains an open pre-launch decision.
- WhatsApp Business is the intended primary direct-contact channel once the dedicated commercial number, account ownership, availability, and response process are approved.
- A telephone call using the dedicated commercial number is the intended future alternative to WhatsApp Business.
- Roberto's personal telephone number is private and must not be published on the website or recorded in this brief.
- The pickup address must not appear until verified for public visits.
- Each contact route needs ownership, availability expectations, privacy information, and a response process.

### Technical considerations for a later sprint

- data minimization and privacy notice;
- lawful retention and deletion process;
- accessible validation and confirmation;
- rate limiting, honeypot or other proportionate anti-spam controls;
- server-side validation;
- no third-party tracking without approval and consent analysis;
- failure handling that retains a useful alternative contact path.

## 18. Prudent use of generated images

Generated imagery must never substitute for proof of food, equipment, events, people, suppliers, venue capability, or completed work.

### Potentially acceptable after approval

- non-factual mood boards during design exploration;
- abstract textures or atmospheric backgrounds that do not depict the service;
- storyboard frames used internally to plan a real shoot;
- clearly illustrative diagrams where a diagram is more useful than a photograph.

### Not acceptable as factual website evidence

- generated portraits of Roberto or staff;
- generated meat, preparations, flames, stand, barbecue, venues, guests, or events;
- altered crowds or portion sizes;
- simulated testimonials, suppliers, farms, awards, certifications, or historical scenes;
- generated technical cutaways presented as the real system;
- generated imagery intended to imply an event actually occurred.

Every generated asset requires approval, provenance recording, accessibility review, and a check that users cannot reasonably mistake it for documentary evidence. Real photography should supply Open Graph and primary conversion imagery once available.

## 21. Scientific evidence and claim limits

### What the evidence supports generally

- NCI explains that HCAs can form through reactions in muscle meat at high temperatures, while PAHs can form when fat and juices drip onto a heated surface or open fire, generating flames and smoke that may deposit PAHs on meat.
- NCI also states that formation varies with food, method, temperature, time, and doneness, and that population studies have not established a definitive causal link between dietary HCA/PAH exposure from cooked meat and human cancer.
- EFSA's 2008 CONTAM communication identifies incomplete combustion and heat-induced decomposition as PAH sources, notes that some home cooking practices can contribute, and says preventing fat from dropping into flames may reduce exposure during barbecuing. The page itself warns that it is old and may not reflect current information.
- Lee et al. studied pork and beef in specific conventional grilling arrangements. Removing drippings or smoke was associated with lower measured PAH4 in those experimental treatments. The reported percentages cannot be transferred to different equipment without testing.
- The Kang et al. review concerns HCA mechanisms and mitigation research. External fat collection primarily relates to a PAH smoke pathway and does not establish control of HCA formation.
- Sumer and Oz found PAHs at varying levels in both direct and indirect methods in their particular study and did not find a statistically significant method effect on summed PAH4 or PAH8, although the highest values occurred in well-done meat cooked directly. This reinforces that equipment labels alone do not guarantee absence.

### Claim framework

| Claim class                       | Example                                                                     | Rule                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mechanical characteristic         | Liquids are conveyed outside the chamber                                    | Publish after current-equipment verification and photography                              |
| Operational benefit               | Liquids do not fall directly onto the heat source below the food            | Publish after technical confirmation                                                      |
| Gastronomic observation           | A selected wood contributes a particular aroma                              | Attribute to actual practice; avoid universal outcome claims                              |
| General scientific context        | Fat dripping onto flame is one pathway associated with PAH-containing smoke | Cite carefully and include limitations if used                                            |
| Product-specific analytical claim | The system reduces PAH by a stated percentage                               | Prohibited without a product-specific comparative study                                   |
| Health claim                      | Healthier, non-toxic, PAH-free, HCA-free, more digestible                   | Prohibited without appropriate legal and scientific substantiation; currently unavailable |

### If product testing is ever considered

Define the current equipment, foods, fat content, fuel, wood, temperatures, duration, doneness, sampling, comparator, replicate count, PAH4/PAH8 and relevant HCA methods, accredited laboratory, statistical plan, and publication wording before testing. Independent technical and legal review would be required. A favorable result for one protocol would not support universal claims.
